Research work · AEIV Global · 2026

A comparative study of Document Intelligence platforms

My research work examined how AI systems extract, classify and organize information from unstructured documents. The comparative review covered research models including LayoutLM, LayoutLMv2, DocFormer, GraphLayoutLM and LayoutLLM, alongside cloud services such as Microsoft Azure Document Intelligence, Amazon Textract and Google Document AI.

The study focused on a practical selection problem: platform capability alone is not enough. Teams also need to consider document-type fit, integration with existing enterprise systems, extraction reliability, privacy and personally identifiable information (PII) governance, and the limitations of LLM-based extraction such as hallucinations. The paper proposed a structured, five-phase framework to help practitioners compare and select tools for their use case.

From research into applied security: AutoVAPT

I also contributed to AutoVAPT, an automated vulnerability assessment and penetration-testing platform. Its architecture connected a Streamlit dashboard, Python/FastAPI services and PostgreSQL, with Docker Compose coordinating the components. The workflow processed scan results through normalization and deduplication, then used CVSS-based severity interpretation to make findings easier to review.

According to my internship report, five integration checks passed: dashboard accessibility, API communication, database persistence, Docker service integration, and risk-score/severity classification. These checks provided a baseline validation of the containerized workflow.

Research themes: Document AI · Information extraction · Responsible data handling · Application security · Vulnerability assessment · Microservices
